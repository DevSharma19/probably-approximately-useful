Dropping realizability

Now remove the realizability assumption. The labels may be noisy, or no hypothesis in $\mathcal{H}$ may be perfect. We can no longer ask for error $\le \varepsilon$. Instead, we ask to be nearly as good as the best hypothesis in the class.

$\mathcal{H}$ is agnostic PAC learnable if there exist $m_{\mathcal{H}} : (0,1)^2 \to \mathbb{N}$ and an algorithm $A$ such that for every $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0,1)$ and every distribution $\mathcal{D}$ over $\mathcal{X} \times \{0,1\}$: given $m \ge m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta)$ i.i.d. examples from $\mathcal{D}$, $A$ outputs $h$ with $$\Pr_{S \sim \mathcal{D}^m}\Big[L_{\mathcal{D}}(h) \le \min_{h' \in \mathcal{H}} L_{\mathcal{D}}(h') + \varepsilon\Big] \ge 1 - \delta.$$

When realizability holds, $\min_{h'} L_{\mathcal{D}}(h') = 0$ and this is exactly PAC learnability from Chapter 1.

Uniform convergence

ERM works when the empirical error $L_S(h)$ is close to the true error $L_{\mathcal{D}}(h)$ for every $h \in \mathcal{H}$ at the same time, not just for one fixed $h$.

$S$ is $\varepsilon$-representative if $|L_S(h) - L_{\mathcal{D}}(h)| \le \varepsilon$ for all $h \in \mathcal{H}$.
$\mathcal{H}$ has the uniform convergence property if there is $m^{UC}_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta)$ such that for every $\mathcal{D}$, a sample of size $m \ge m^{UC}_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta)$ is $\varepsilon$-representative with probability at least $1-\delta$.
If $S$ is $\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}$-representative, then any ERM output $h_S$ satisfies $L_{\mathcal{D}}(h_S) \le \min_{h\in\mathcal{H}} L_{\mathcal{D}}(h) + \varepsilon$.
