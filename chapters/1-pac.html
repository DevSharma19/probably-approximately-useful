1. Learning from Samples

The setup

A domain $\mathcal{X}$, labels $\{0,1\}$, an unknown distribution $\mathcal{D}$ over $\mathcal{X}$, and an unknown target $f : \mathcal{X} \to \{0,1\}$. The learner sees a sample $S = ((x_1, f(x_1)), \dots, (x_m, f(x_m)))$ with $x_i \sim \mathcal{D}$ i.i.d., and outputs a hypothesis $h$.

$L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h) = \Pr_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}[h(x) \neq f(x)]$ and $L_S(h) = \frac{1}{m}\left|\{ i : h(x_i) \neq f(x_i)\}\right|$.
A hypothesis class $\mathcal{H}$ is PAC learnable if there exist a function $m_{\mathcal{H}} : (0,1)^2 \to \mathbb{N}$ and a learning algorithm $A$ such that for every $\varepsilon, \delta \in (0,1)$, every distribution $\mathcal{D}$ over $\mathcal{X}$, and every target $f \in \mathcal{H}$: given $m \ge m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta)$ i.i.d. samples labeled by $f$, $A$ outputs $h$ with $$\Pr_{S \sim \mathcal{D}^m}\big[L_{\mathcal{D},f}(h) \le \varepsilon\big] \ge 1 - \delta.$$

Empirical Risk Minimization

$\mathrm{ERM}_{\mathcal{H}}$: output any $h \in \mathcal{H}$ minimizing $L_S(h)$.

If $\mathcal{H}$ is finite, ERM is a PAC learner with sample complexity $$m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta) \le \left\lceil \frac{\ln(|\mathcal{H}|/\delta)}{\varepsilon} \right\rceil.$$
