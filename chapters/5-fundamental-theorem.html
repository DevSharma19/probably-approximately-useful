5. The Fundamental Theorem of PAC Learning

For a class $\mathcal{H}$ of functions $\mathcal{X} \to \{0,1\}$ with 0-1 loss, the following are equivalent:
  1. $\mathcal{H}$ has the uniform convergence property.
  2. Any ERM rule is a successful agnostic PAC learner for $\mathcal{H}$.
  3. $\mathcal{H}$ is agnostic PAC learnable.
  4. $\mathcal{H}$ is PAC learnable.
  5. Any ERM rule is a successful PAC learner for $\mathcal{H}$.
  6. $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) < \infty$.
If $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) = d < \infty$, there are absolute constants $C_1, C_2$ such that, in the realizable case, $$\begin{aligned}m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta) &\ge C_1 \frac{d + \log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon}, \\ m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta) &\le C_2 \frac{d \log(1/\varepsilon) + \log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon}.\end{aligned}$$ In the agnostic case, $$\begin{aligned}m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta) &\ge C_1 \frac{d + \log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon^2}, \\ m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta) &\le C_2 \frac{d + \log(1/\delta)}{\varepsilon^2}.\end{aligned}$$

The sample complexity depends on $\mathcal{H}$ only through $d$. Noise costs a factor of about $1/\varepsilon$.

Roadmap

The proof is a cycle of implications. Each arrow is either a result from an earlier chapter or immediate from the definitions.

  • 6 ⇒ 1: Sauer–Shelah plus the VC inequality (Chapters 3–4).
  • 1 ⇒ 2: the "Representative ⇒ ERM succeeds" lemma (Chapter 4).
  • 2 ⇒ 3 and 5 ⇒ 4: by definition.
  • 3 ⇒ 4 and 2 ⇒ 5: under realizability, $\min_h L_{\mathcal{D}}(h) = 0$. So an agnostic guarantee is a realizable guarantee.
  • 4 ⇒ 6: No Free Lunch (below).

Sufficiency: finite VC ⇒ learnable

Let $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) = d < \infty$. Chapter 4 gives uniform convergence with $m^{UC}_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta) = O\big((d\log(1/\varepsilon) + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon^2\big)$. So ERM is an agnostic PAC learner with $m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta) \le m^{UC}_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon/2,\delta)$.

In the realizable case we can do better. ERM outputs a consistent $h$. We only need that no bad $h$ is consistent. The ghost-sample argument from Chapter 4 adapts to this event:

For $m \ge 8/\varepsilon$, $$\begin{aligned}&\Pr_{S \sim \mathcal{D}^m}\big[\exists h \in \mathcal{H} : L_S(h) = 0 \text{ and } L_{\mathcal{D}}(h) > \varepsilon\big] \\ &\qquad\le 2\,\tau_{\mathcal{H}}(2m)\, 2^{-\varepsilon m/2}.\end{aligned}$$

With Sauer–Shelah, this is at most $\delta$ for $m = O\big((d\log(1/\varepsilon) + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon\big)$. This matches the upper bound in the quantitative theorem.

Necessity: infinite VC ⇒ not learnable

Let $A$ be any learner for binary classification over $\mathcal{X}$, and let $m < |\mathcal{X}|/2$. Then there exist a distribution $\mathcal{D}$ and a function $f : \mathcal{X} \to \{0,1\}$ with $L_{\mathcal{D}}(f) = 0$ such that $\Pr_{S \sim \mathcal{D}^m}\big[L_{\mathcal{D}}(A(S)) \ge 1/8\big] \ge 1/7$.

Idea: put $\mathcal{D}$ on a set $C$ of $2m$ points. The sample misses at least half of $C$. On the missed points, the labels can be anything. The learner has no information about them.

Proof

Pick $C \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ with $|C| = 2m$. Let $f_1, \dots, f_T$ be the $T = 2^{2m}$ functions $C \to \{0,1\}$. Let $\mathcal{D}_i$ be uniform over $\{(x, f_i(x)) : x \in C\}$. Then $L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(f_i) = 0$.

Step 1: averaging. There are $k = (2m)^m$ sequences of $m$ points from $C$. Call them $S_1, \dots, S_k$. Let $S_j^i$ be $S_j$ labeled by $f_i$. Each $S_j$ is equally likely under $\mathcal{D}_i^m$. So $$\begin{aligned}&\max_i \mathbb{E}_{S \sim \mathcal{D}_i^m}\big[L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S))\big] \\ &\quad= \max_i \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S_j^i)) \\ &\quad\ge \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^T \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S_j^i)) \\ &\quad= \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^T L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S_j^i)) \\ &\quad\ge \min_j \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^T L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S_j^i)).\end{aligned}$$ The first inequality replaces a max with an average. Then we swap the sums. The last step replaces an average with a min.

Step 2: unseen points. Fix $j$. Let $v_1, \dots, v_p$ be the points of $C$ not in $S_j$. Then $p \ge m$. For any $h$, $$\begin{aligned}L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(h) &= \frac{1}{2m}\sum_{x \in C}\mathbb{1}[h(x) \ne f_i(x)] \\ &\ge \frac{1}{2p}\sum_{r=1}^{p}\mathbb{1}[h(v_r) \ne f_i(v_r)].\end{aligned}$$ The inequality drops the seen points and uses $p \ge m$. Write $h_i = A(S_j^i)$. Then $$\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^T L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(h_i) &\ge \frac{1}{2p}\sum_{r=1}^{p} \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^T \mathbb{1}[h_i(v_r) \ne f_i(v_r)] \\ &\ge \frac{1}{2}\min_r \frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^T \mathbb{1}[h_i(v_r) \ne f_i(v_r)].\end{aligned}$$

Step 3: pairing. Fix $r$. Split the $f_i$ into $T/2$ pairs $(f_i, f_{i'})$ that differ only at $v_r$. The point $v_r$ is not in $S_j$, so $S_j^i = S_j^{i'}$ and $h_i = h_{i'}$. Exactly one of $f_i(v_r)$, $f_{i'}(v_r)$ disagrees with $h_i(v_r)$. So the average over $i$ is exactly $\frac{1}{2}$.

Step 4: from expectation to probability. Steps 1–3 give some $i$ with $\mathbb{E}[L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S))] \ge \frac{1}{4}$. Let $L = L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S))$ and $q = \Pr[L \ge 1/8]$. Since $L \le 1$, $$\tfrac{1}{4} \le \mathbb{E}[L] \le q \cdot 1 + (1 - q)\cdot\tfrac{1}{8}.$$ So $q \ge 1/7$. Take $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{D}_i$ and $f$ any extension of $f_i$ to $\mathcal{X}$. $\blacksquare$

If $\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{H}) = \infty$, then $\mathcal{H}$ is not PAC learnable.
Proof

Suppose $A$ PAC learns $\mathcal{H}$. Set $\varepsilon = 1/9$, $\delta = 1/8$, and $m = m_{\mathcal{H}}(\varepsilon,\delta)$. Some set $C$ of size $2m$ is shattered by $\mathcal{H}$. Run the No Free Lunch proof on $C$. Each $f_i$ agrees on $C$ with some $h \in \mathcal{H}$, so each $\mathcal{D}_i$ is realizable by $\mathcal{H}$.

For the bad $\mathcal{D}_i$, $\Pr[L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S)) \ge 1/8] \ge 1/7$. The PAC guarantee gives $\Pr[L_{\mathcal{D}_i}(A(S)) \le 1/9] \ge 7/8$. These events are disjoint, and $1/7 + 7/8 > 1$. Contradiction. $\blacksquare$

The same argument with a shattered set of size $d$ shows $m_{\mathcal{H}} \ge d/2$ for small constant $\varepsilon, \delta$. The full lower bound $\Omega\big((d + \log(1/\delta))/\varepsilon\big)$ is due to Ehrenfeucht, Haussler, Kearns, and Valiant (1989).
